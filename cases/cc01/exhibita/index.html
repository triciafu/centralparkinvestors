PRE-CONTRACT WRITTEN ASSURANCE AND LATER POSITION

On October 10, 2025, the Customer objected in writing to Section 8 (Limitation of Damages) of the Proposal and Contract (the “Contract”) and proposed revised language before signing. On October 15, 2025, the Company, through its Vice President and relayed by its Designer, acknowledged that this concern was “preventing you from signing the contract” and confirmed in writing: “we are responsible for any and all damages directly caused by our employees during the installation which would be covered under our insurance policy.” The email gave the example of striking a water pipe behind the wall, stating that “ALL damages as a result would be covered under our insurance policy,” and added that “we cover any damages we have caused.”

That evening, the Customer’s project contact replied that the Customer would sign given the additional clarification and the confirmation of insurance and damage coverage. The Contract was executed on October 16, 2025.

On January 30, 2026, the Company’s Sales Manager concluded, following an on-site review of the installation, that the excessive gaps and blocked outlets could not be addressed without removal, and on February 11, 2026, the Company presented replacement, refund, or removal options. The Customer accepted removal and a refund. The Company then proposed revised terms disclaiming responsibility for damage of the kind covered by the October 15, 2025 assurance, and for wall repair, repainting, and restoration. The Customer declined those terms, and no written modification under Section 12 of the Contract was executed. On March 18, 2026, the Company withdrew the removal and refund proposal and requested payment of the $9,545.00 balance.
